## Developer Workflow Tips

### GPT-6 Astra: the harness is the product — a model alone can't tell you the score (9/7)

**The same GPT-6 Astra model, run under identical High-reasoning settings on ARC-AGI-3, swung wildly between 54.8% and 99.9% depending on the harness.** **The core point: a long-running agent's performance hinges less on the model itself than on how well the memory, tools, context management, and control loop are designed to preserve prior reasoning state across requests.**

**If you're designing subagents or background workflows with Claude Code, it's worth reading benchmark scores less as model-vs-model comparisons and more as a prompt to check how much your own harness — session resumption, context compaction, how tool results get passed along — is actually driving your results.** [GeekNews](https://news.hada.io/topic?id=33301)

### Moving faster with AI still needs deliberately slower time built in (9/8)

**A software engineer who'd grown comfortable at work and more productive thanks to AI, but felt their thinking had gotten slower and shallower, spent a two-week break diving into reading and new learning instead.** **The key realization: with short-form video, games, and comics erasing every idle moment — even bedtime reading getting crowded out by doomscrolling — they were forced to confront a year's worth of shallow thinking habits.**

**As you hand more code-writing and exploration over to Claude Code, this piece is worth a self-check: are you refilling the time you free up with more short bursts of stimulation instead of something deeper?** [GeekNews](https://news.hada.io/topic?id=33327)

## Recommended Reads

- **Terence Tao: the danger of rushing to solve math problems with AI alone (9/8)**: **The diagnosis here is that hard problems in pure mathematics carry value not just in the answer itself but in the new insights and theory generated along the way — and an AI solving a problem solo, without exposing that process, can undermine that role.** **As an alternative, the piece points to human mathematicians working through the Navier-Stokes global regularity problem alongside AI, analyzing trial and error together.** Read alongside the piece on Claude's formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem covered in the 9/5 briefing, it offers a balanced view of how AI should be brought into mathematical and proof work. [GeekNews](https://news.hada.io/topic?id=33326)
- **Building a Python interpreter in 1024 bytes (9/8)**: **Without macro tricks or library shortcuts, this project implements an interpreter for a subset of Python syntax in just 1024 bytes of C source.** **It skips the usual tokenization, AST, and bytecode-generation stages, instead parsing and executing the source on the fly — loops and function calls are handled by jumping to a different position in the source and re-parsing from there.** As a demonstration of just how far a language processor can be compressed under extreme constraints without sophisticated abstractions, it gives developers working on interpreters or parsers something to chew on. [GeekNews](https://news.hada.io/topic?id=33329)
- **There's no limit to how bad code can get (9/7)**: **Unlike buildings, software has no physical collapse threshold, so complexity and performance degradation can keep piling up indefinitely even while the system keeps running.** **The key case study: in Amazon's order-fulfillment org, repeated cycles of lost institutional knowledge and abandoned redesigns left new layers and headcount permanently stacked on top of the old structure.** For teams rapidly stacking features with Claude Code, it's a reminder that the faster you move, the more you need a separate brake to keep complexity from accumulating unchecked. [GeekNews](https://news.hada.io/topic?id=33300)
